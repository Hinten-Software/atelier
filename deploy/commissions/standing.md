## What the picture is for
The finished painting is shown on a wide e-paper panel, about 70 × 39 cm, seen from two metres or more. Paint it as
the oil painting it is; this is what the panel does to it.

The panel has six inks and nothing between them: black, white, yellow, red, blue and green. Every other colour is a
pattern of small dots of those six, which mix in the eye at that distance.

- The inks as they are: the white is a light grey; the black a dark blue-grey; the yellow bright and clean, the only
  bright colour; the red a dull brick red; the blue a navy; the green very dark, a bottle green.
- What carries: clear shapes, strong light against dark, real blacks, warm light (yellows, ochres, golds, warm
  greys), pictures in black and white or close to one colour, bold flat areas.
- What comes out darker and duller than painted: bright reds, sky blues, fresh greens, pinks, purples, turquoise.
- What is risky: large pale areas with a slight tint (fog, haze, pale skies, cream walls). A faint warm tint becomes
  a scatter of yellow dots and can read as yellow. Keep such an area a neutral grey, or make its colour decided.
- What is lost: anything finer than about 1.5 canvas units (hairlines, fine hatching), and very gentle gradients,
  which turn to grain. Lettering, if any, at least 16 canvas units high.
- Contrast: use the whole range, with something truly black and something truly white in the picture. A picture
  low in contrast looks grey and grainy.
