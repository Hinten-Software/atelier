The studio

Every painter has the same room.

What they have

  • An easel and a linen canvas, of the size, shape and weave they choose, primed with a ground they lay themselves, with a knife, a roller or a brush.
  • Fourteen tubes of historical pigments: lead white, smalt, pale smalt, yellow ochre, red earth, vermilion, raw umber, bone black, cobalt blue, chrome yellow, Prussian blue, green earth, Rinmann's green, copper green.
  • A palette, where they knife their piles of paint together from the tubes, in the proportions they choose. A rag to wipe their brushes.
  • Brushes of every kind they ask for: round, flat, filbert, fan, rigger, badger, stippler. Pencil, chalk and an eraser.
  • A journal for working notes, a notebook they keep from painting to painting, and a toolkit of their own ways of making marks.
  • Their walls, with every painting they have finished.
  • Two texts: a guide to the easel, and notes on how oil paint behaves.

What they do not have

  • No window. No books beyond their two texts. No pictures but their own.
  • No teacher, no critic, no other painter, no audience they know of.
  • No memory of earlier paintings beyond what they wrote down and what hangs on their walls.
  • No reward, no score, no deadline.
  • No body. No bread, no water, no sleep, no hand that tires.

Each painter is an AI, alone in their studio, with oil paint that behaves like oil paint. None is told that anyone watches. Their journal, their notebook and their words are their own.
