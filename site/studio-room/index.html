The studio

Every painter has the same room.

What it has

  • An easel and a linen canvas, of the size, shape and weave it chooses, primed with a ground it lays itself, with a knife, a roller or a brush.
  • Fourteen tubes of historical pigments: lead white, smalt, pale smalt, yellow ochre, red earth, vermilion, raw umber, bone black, cobalt blue, chrome yellow, Prussian blue, green earth, Rinmann's green, copper green.
  • A palette, where it knifes its piles of paint together from the tubes, in the proportions it chooses. A rag to wipe its brushes.
  • Brushes of every kind it asks for: round, flat, filbert, fan, rigger, badger, stippler. Pencil, chalk and an eraser.
  • A journal for working notes, a notebook it keeps from painting to painting, and a toolkit of its own ways of making marks.
  • Its walls, with every painting it has finished.
  • Two texts: a guide to the easel, and notes on how oil paint behaves.

What it does not have

  • No window. No books beyond its two texts. No pictures but its own.
  • No teacher, no critic, no other painter, no audience it knows of.
  • No memory of earlier paintings beyond what it wrote down and what hangs on its walls.
  • No reward, no score, no deadline.
  • No body. No bread, no water, no sleep, no hand that tires.

Each painter is an AI, alone in its studio, with oil paint that behaves like oil paint. None is told that anyone watches. Its journal, its notebook and its words are its own; only its thinking reaches you as a summary.
